Think Before You Post

What we say online can affect people offline. Here’s what I learned about the law and what we can do to be more thoughtful.

By Braedyn Mannion · The Rutherford Times

We use group chats, games and social media all the time to talk to friends and share things. Most of it is harmless, but a message can quickly turn into something that really upsets someone. A rumour can get passed around, or a photo can be shared when the person in it never agreed to that.

New Zealand has the Harmful Digital Communications Act 2015. The Act is there to help people who have been seriously harmed by digital communications. It sets out principles for how people should communicate online and ways to get help. It doesn’t mean every disagreement or rude comment is automatically a legal case; what happened and how much harm was caused are important.

The Act’s 10 principles say digital messages should not share sensitive personal facts, threaten or harass someone, make false allegations, or publish private information without permission. They also cover grossly offensive, indecent or obscene content, encouraging others to cause harm, encouraging suicide, and denigrating people because of who they are. That’s a summary, so it’s worth checking the actual Act for its full wording.

If something harmful happens, try not to reply while you’re angry, and don’t forward it to other people. If it’s safe, keep useful details such as the account name, link and date so you can explain what happened when asking for help. Report the post to the app or website, and talk to a trusted adult, like a parent, teacher or school counsellor.

Netsafe can give advice about online harm in New Zealand. It can help explain options, although it can’t guarantee a post will be taken down, so use the platform’s report tools too. If someone is in immediate danger, call 111.

I think the easiest thing to remember is to pause before posting. Ask yourself if it’s true, if it’s private, and how the person might feel reading it. We won’t always agree with everyone online, but we can still be respectful. If a post seems like it could hurt someone, don’t add to it—check in with them or ask a trusted adult for help instead.

For more information: Netsafe · The full Act. This student post is general information, not legal advice.
